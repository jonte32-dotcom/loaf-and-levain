# Scoring sourdough: ear, bloom, and the basic patterns

A scored loaf opens where you tell it to. An unscored loaf tears wherever the dough is weakest — usually a ragged blowout along the side, sometimes a flat top with no rise at all. Sourdough scoring is how you control that explosive expansion in the first 10 minutes of baking, when steam and trapped CO₂ are pushing the crumb upward against a crust that's rapidly setting.

Done well, you get an ear (a raised lip of crust along the cut) and bloom (the dramatic opening and lift around the score). Done poorly, the cut closes back up, the loaf goes flat, or the crust splits sideways. Most fixes are mechanical, not mystical.

## The tools

A real lame with a fresh double-edged razor blade. Not a paring knife, not a serrated bread knife, not a butter knife. The blade needs to glide through wet dough without dragging. A dull blade snags, deflates the surface, and gives you a closed-up score every time.

Swap blades every 5–10 loaves. They cost pennies. A blade that scored beautifully last week may be dull enough today to ruin a bake.

## What temperature dough scores best

Cold. After a 12–16 hour cold retard at 38°F (3°C), the surface is firm and tacky but not sticky. The blade slices cleanly instead of dragging through soft, gassy dough.

If you're baking same-day without a cold retard, chill the shaped loaf in the freezer for 20–30 minutes before scoring. The skin firms up enough to take a clean cut.

## Angle and depth

Two cuts, two purposes:

- **Ear cut**: blade held at roughly 20–30° from the surface, almost parallel to the loaf. This creates a flap of dough that lifts during oven spring — that's your ear. Depth: about 1/4 inch (6 mm).
- **Decorative cut**: blade held at 90°, straight down. Used for wheat stalks, leaves, and patterns where you want clean lines, not dramatic lift. Depth: 1/8 inch (3 mm), shallower.

Most beginners cut too deep and too vertical. A vertical cut 1/2 inch deep just opens up and stays open — no ear, just a canyon. A shallow angled cut produces the lift.

## The basic patterns

### Single slash (the workhorse)

One off-center cut running 3/4 of the length of the loaf, at a 30° angle. This is the classic batard score. It directs all the oven spring into one ear and gives you maximum bloom in one direction. If you bake one loaf shape, learn this one first.

### Cross or square

Two perpendicular cuts on a boule. Vertical, not angled. Produces four "petals" that open evenly. Forgiving — works even if your shaping is mediocre because expansion is distributed.

### Wheat stalk

A central spine cut down the loaf, then short angled cuts branching off either side. All shallow (about 1/8 inch), all at 90°. Decorative, not structural — pair it with a single deep ear cut along one side if you want bloom too.

### Coil or spiral on a boule

One continuous curving cut from center outward. Looks impressive, scores forgivingly. Keep it shallow.

## Why your loaves aren't blooming

Run through this list before blaming the score:

1. **Underproofed**: dough has nowhere to expand from internally — score opens but the crumb stays dense. Look for a crumb that bloomed but with a tight, gummy interior.
2. **Overproofed**: no spring left. The score will sit flat or even spread sideways. Crumb will be open but flat overall.
3. **Weak gluten / poor shaping**: surface tension is what holds the loaf upright while it springs. A slack, sloppy shape blows out the bottom.
4. **Not enough steam**: without steam in the first 15 minutes, the crust sets before bloom can happen. A Dutch oven solves this; an open-bake without a steam source rarely produces a strong ear.
5. **Oven not hot enough**: 475–500°F (245–260°C) for the covered phase. A 425°F oven won't drive enough spring.

If you're not sure whether your dough is at the right hydration to hold a clean score, the [hydration calculator](#calculator) on this page can help you dial in a recipe — wetter doughs (78%+) need firmer chilling and a sharper blade; drier doughs (68–72%) score easily but spring less dramatically.

## What to do today

1. Buy a pack of double-edged razor blades. Replace yours.
2. Cold-retard your next loaf overnight, score it straight from the fridge.
3. Make one off-center slash, blade at 30°, 1/4 inch deep, in one confident motion.
4. Bake covered at 500°F for 20 minutes, then uncovered at 450°F until done.

One variable at a time. If the ear shows up, you've got your baseline — start playing with patterns from there.
